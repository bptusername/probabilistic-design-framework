Methodological note. Water demand per active cell is treated separately as an internal variable because operational consumption can change while the territorial land-use configuration and protection priorities remain unchanged. Agriculture, urban and recreational demand can therefore be redefined at each temporal interval. The default values are illustrative rather than calibrated real-world coefficients. A restricted cell contributes zero demand during that interval. Water-collection cells are persistent and each stores up to 8 water units for the following interval.

03

Probabilistic transformation

current-state priorities + water balance → stochastic spatial allocation
Dt = Σ Ni,t · qi,t  ·  Wt = At + Rt  ·  ΔW = max(0, Dt − Wt)
1 · Territorial demandDt is the sum of active cells Ni,t multiplied by the internal water-demand variable qi,t assigned to each land-use category.
2 · Water availableWt combines external water availability At with any stored reserve inherited from the previous state Rt.
3 · Deficit to restrictΔW is the shortage that remains when demand exceeds available water. If there is no shortage, ΔW = 0 and surplus water may instead be stored.

The model first calculates territorial demand from the current land-use state and the three internal water-demand variables, then compares this demand with external water availability and inherited reserve. Any resulting deficit is distributed among land uses according to the protection priorities. These quantities define the transition; the seed determines the sampled spatial location of affected cells.

T0 → T1 produces one stochastic realization. The same water balance can produce other spatial allocations when the seed changes; this interface follows one sampled path at a time.
Water-collector siting rule. When surplus water requires new storage capacity, any agricultural, urban or recreational cell may be converted into a collector. Suitability is influenced first by the protection priority assigned to its current land use: lower protection means greater susceptibility to conversion. Among otherwise suitable cells, locations further from existing or newly selected collectors receive a higher score, encouraging a distributed territorial storage network. The seed introduces variation among high-scoring candidates, so the number of collectors is fixed by the water surplus while their exact location can vary between realizations. This is an illustrative design heuristic rather than a calibrated hydrological model.
